# POL-KYC-01 — Customer Due Diligence and Beneficial Ownership

> Regulatory basis: Law no. 43-05 on combating money laundering and terrorist financing (as amended, notably by law no. 12-18); Bank Al-Maghrib Circular no. 5/W/2017 on the vigilance obligation of credit institutions (amended by Circulars 3/W/2019 and 3/W/2024); BAM Directive no. 2/W/2019 on identifying business relationships, occasional customers and beneficial owners; BAM Directive no. 3/W/2019 on the risk-based approach; BAM Directive no. 6/W/2021 on group-wide vigilance; EBA Guidelines on loan origination and monitoring (EBA/GL/2020/06) §4.3.3 and Annex 2.
> Each section states the REGULATORY REQUIREMENT, then the INTERNAL CALIBRATION the bank applies. Internal calibrations are the bank's choice and must be approved by the Board risk committee; they are not set by Bank Al-Maghrib.

## 1. Identification of legal entities
Regulatory requirement: before entering into a business relationship or granting credit, the bank must identify and verify the legal entity and the natural persons who act on its behalf (BAM Circular 5/W/2017; BAM Directive 2/W/2019). A relationship cannot be opened, and no credit can be granted, when identification cannot be completed.
Internal calibration: every corporate customer must provide its statutes (statuts), a commercial register extract (modèle J) dated less than 3 months, its ICE number, its tax identification and a tax certificate (attestation fiscale), and the identity document (CNIE or passport) of every director, manager (gérant) and legal representative, plus the powers of the persons signing the credit request. Files missing any of these documents cannot be approved.

## 2. Beneficial owners (UBO)
Regulatory requirement: the beneficial owner is any natural person who holds, directly or indirectly at the end of the chain, 25% or more of the capital or voting rights of the company. When no person meets this test, or there is doubt, the beneficial owner is every natural person who exercises control over the company by any other means, de jure or de facto (definition used for the Moroccan public register of beneficial owners). The bank must identify and take reasonable measures to verify every beneficial owner (BAM Directive 2/W/2019).
Internal calibration: when a shareholder is a legal entity, the bank looks through the ownership chain, multiplying percentages at each level, until natural persons are identified. A certified UBO declaration is required whenever a shareholder is a company or a beneficial owner is non-resident. If no beneficial owner can be identified, the relationship is escalated to Compliance and cannot be approved at branch or business-line level. The ownership structure is kept in the credit file (EBA/GL/2020/06 Annex 2, item B.18).

## 3. Offshore and opaque structures
Regulatory requirement: enhanced vigilance applies to high-risk customers and to complex or unusual structures with no apparent economic rationale (BAM Circular 5/W/2017; BAM Directive 3/W/2019 risk-based approach). Business relationships linked to jurisdictions that the FATF subjects to a call for action (as of the June 2026 plenary: Iran, DPRK, Myanmar) require enhanced due diligence, and countermeasures for Iran and DPRK.
Internal calibration: shareholders incorporated in high-secrecy jurisdictions (for example British Virgin Islands, Panama, Seychelles, Cayman Islands) or in a jurisdiction on the FATF list of jurisdictions under increased monitoring trigger enhanced due diligence. The analyst must obtain the register of shareholders of the offshore entity, a certified UBO declaration, and a written explanation of the economic rationale of the structure. The British Virgin Islands are on the FATF increased-monitoring list since 2025; the list must be refreshed after every FATF plenary (February, June, October).

## 4. Newly incorporated companies
Regulatory requirement: the risk-based approach requires the bank to adjust vigilance to the customer's risk profile (BAM Directive 3/W/2019). For credit, the bank must assess the borrower's experience and ability to manage the activity financed and the feasibility of its business plan (EBA/GL/2020/06 §5.2).
Internal calibration: companies incorporated less than 24 months ago with limited financial history are higher risk. Credit requests above 5,000,000 MAD from such companies require enhanced due diligence, a business plan with financial projections, a review of the sponsors' track record, and a site visit report.

## 5. Risk classification and review frequency
Regulatory requirement: the bank must classify customers by ML/TF risk, apply simplified measures only where risk is low (for example listed companies and regulated Moroccan entities) and enhanced measures to high-risk customers, and keep customer information up to date throughout the relationship (BAM Circular 5/W/2017; BAM Directive 3/W/2019). Credit files of counterparties belonging to groups whose bank debt is 500 MDH or more must be updated at least once a year (BAM Directive 2/W/16, as reported).
Internal calibration: customers are classified Low, Medium or High risk. High-risk customers are reviewed every 12 months, Medium every 24 months and Low every 36 months, and at any trigger event (change of shareholder, director, sanctions or adverse media alert). High-risk onboarding or credit decisions require approval by the Compliance Officer.

## 6. Sector inherent risk
Regulatory requirement: the bank must assess ML/TF risk by customer type, product, geography and distribution channel (BAM Directive 3/W/2019; EBA/GL/2020/06 §4.3.3). The national ML/TF risk assessment and BAM's sector risk mapping are inputs to this classification.
Internal calibration: real estate development, general import/export trading, money exchange, precious metals and cash-intensive businesses are classified as high inherent-risk sectors. Logistics and construction are medium. Agriculture, manufacturing with audited accounts, and regulated professions are low.

## 7. Purpose of the credit and source of repayment
Regulatory requirement: for each credit, the bank must understand the purpose of the loan, assess whether any third party associated with the facility creates ML/TF risk, and take risk-sensitive measures to confirm that the funds used to repay come from legitimate sources (EBA/GL/2020/06 §4.3.3).
Internal calibration: the purpose of the credit must be stated in the request and be consistent with the declared activity. Repayment by, or guarantees from, third parties with no documented economic link to the borrower require Compliance review.
