# POL-KYC-01 — Customer Due Diligence and Beneficial Ownership

> MOCK internal policy for demonstration. Replace with your bank's approved procedures. It is written in the spirit of Moroccan AML law no. 43-05 and Bank Al-Maghrib vigilance requirements, but it is not a legal reference.

## 1. Identification of legal entities
Every corporate customer must provide its statutes, a recent extract of the commercial register (modèle J), its ICE number, and the identity documents (CNIE or passport) of every director and legal representative. Files missing any of these documents cannot be approved.

## 2. Beneficial owners (UBO)
The bank must identify every natural person who directly or indirectly owns 25% or more of the capital or voting rights, or who otherwise exercises control. When a shareholder is a legal entity, the bank must look through the ownership chain until the natural persons are identified. If no beneficial owner can be identified, the relationship must be escalated to Compliance and cannot be approved at branch or business-line level.

## 3. Offshore and opaque structures
Shareholders incorporated in high-secrecy jurisdictions (for example British Virgin Islands, Panama, Seychelles, Cayman Islands) trigger enhanced due diligence. The analyst must obtain the register of shareholders of the offshore entity, a certified UBO declaration, and an explanation of the economic rationale of the structure.

## 4. Newly incorporated companies
Companies incorporated less than 24 months ago with limited financial history are considered higher risk. Credit requests above 5,000,000 MAD from such companies require enhanced due diligence and a site visit report.

## 5. Risk classification and review frequency
Customers are classified Low, Medium or High risk. High-risk customers are reviewed every 12 months, Medium every 24 months and Low every 36 months. High-risk onboarding or credit decisions require approval by the Compliance Officer.

## 6. Sector inherent risk
Real estate development, general import/export trading, money exchange, precious metals and cash-intensive businesses are classified as high inherent-risk sectors. Logistics and construction are medium. Agriculture, manufacturing with audited accounts, and regulated professions are low.
