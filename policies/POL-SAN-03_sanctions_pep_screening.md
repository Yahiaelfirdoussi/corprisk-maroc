# POL-SAN-03 — Sanctions, PEP and Adverse Media Screening

> Regulatory basis: Law no. 43-05 as amended (targeted financial sanctions, asset freezing); UN Security Council resolutions implemented in Morocco through the national commission CNASNU (Commission nationale chargée de l'application des sanctions prévues par les résolutions du Conseil de sécurité), which maintains the national list and handles freezing; BAM Circular no. 5/W/2017 as amended (vigilance, politically exposed persons); FATF Recommendations 6, 7 and 12.
> Each section states the REGULATORY REQUIREMENT, then the INTERNAL CALIBRATION the bank applies.

## 1. Scope of screening
Regulatory requirement: the bank must check customers and the persons linked to them against the UN Security Council consolidated sanctions list and the national list maintained by the CNASNU, before the relationship starts and whenever the lists change.
Internal calibration: the company, its directors, shareholders, beneficial owners, guarantors and its main transaction counterparties are screened against the UN consolidated list, the national list, the bank's internal blacklist, the PEP database and, where the bank has exposure to those currencies or markets, the EU and US (OFAC) lists. Screening is repeated at every list update and at each credit review.

## 2. Sanctions matches
Regulatory requirement: funds and assets of designated persons or entities must be frozen without delay and the competent authorities informed; no funds or financial services may be made available to them (law 43-05 as amended; CNASNU).
Internal calibration: a confirmed or probable match results in an immediate block of the relationship and of any pending transaction, and mandatory escalation to Compliance. Assets are frozen where required by law. No credit or onboarding decision can be made until Compliance has cleared the match.

## 3. Potential matches and false positives
Regulatory requirement: the bank must have procedures to resolve alerts and keep records of the checks performed (law 43-05, record-keeping obligations).
Internal calibration: name similarity alone does not confirm a match. Potential matches must be reviewed by an analyst who compares date of birth, nationality, address and identifiers (CNIE, passport, ICE, RC). The review and its conclusion are documented in the file and kept for at least 10 years.

## 4. Politically exposed persons (PEP)
Regulatory requirement: when a customer or beneficial owner is a politically exposed person, the bank must apply enhanced vigilance: senior management approval of the relationship, measures to establish the source of wealth and source of funds, and enhanced ongoing monitoring (BAM Circular 5/W/2017; FATF Recommendation 12). Family members and close associates of PEPs are covered.
Internal calibration: a director, shareholder, beneficial owner or guarantor who is, or was within the last 5 years, a PEP — or a family member or close associate of one — triggers enhanced due diligence, source of wealth and source of funds verification, and senior management approval.

## 5. Internal blacklist
Regulatory requirement: none specific; the blacklist implements the bank's own risk appetite and past suspicious transaction reports.
Internal calibration: any match with the internal blacklist requires Compliance review before any new product or credit is granted.

## 6. Adverse media
Regulatory requirement: the risk-based approach requires the bank to take into account all relevant information on the customer's reputation (BAM Directive 3/W/2019). For credit, EBA/GL/2020/06 lists reputational damage among the events to consider in the borrower's sensitivity analysis and major litigation among the information to collect (Annex 2, item B.11).
Internal calibration: negative press related to fraud, corruption, money laundering, tax evasion, environmental damage or consumer complaints, and major litigation, must be documented and assessed for reputational and credit risk. Corruption-related findings are also assessed under BAM Directive 1/W/2022 on corruption risk.
