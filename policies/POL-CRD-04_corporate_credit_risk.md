# POL-CRD-04 — Corporate Credit Risk Appetite

> Regulatory basis: Basel Committee Principles for the Management of Credit Risk (2000, revised April 2025) — Principle 4, banks must operate within sound, well-defined credit-granting criteria; EBA Guidelines on loan origination and monitoring (EBA/GL/2020/06) §5.2 and Annexes 1 and 3; ECB Guidance on leveraged transactions (May 2017); BAM Directive no. 1/G/2005 on minimum information for credit files; BAM Circular no. 8/G/2012 on the maximum risk-division ratio; BAM Directive no. 48/G/2007 on credit concentration risk.
> Regulators require banks to set and document credit-granting criteria and metric limits; except where a section cites a BAM ratio, the limits below are the bank's INTERNAL CALIBRATION within its risk appetite, approved by the Board.

## 1. Leverage
Regulatory requirement: the creditworthiness assessment must consider the borrower's leverage, capital structure and dividend policy (EBA/GL/2020/06 §5.2), and credit-granting criteria must include acceptable leverage and debt-to-equity limits (Annex 1, items 17-18).
Internal calibration: the debt-to-equity ratio (total liabilities / equity) should not exceed 3.0. Above 3.0 the file requires additional guarantees or a reduced exposure. Negative equity is outside risk appetite without a recovery plan (see §4).

## 2. Liquidity
Regulatory requirement: the assessment must cover working capital and the cash conversion cycle, to identify working capital needs (EBA/GL/2020/06 §5.2; Annex 3 coverage ratio, current assets / short-term debt).
Internal calibration: the current ratio (current assets / current liabilities) should be at least 1.0. A ratio below 1.0 indicates working capital pressure and requires a review of the cash-flow plan and of short-term facilities.

## 3. Debt service capacity
Regulatory requirement: the assessment must focus on the borrower's current and future cash flows and its ability to repay; collateral is a second way out and must not be the predominant criterion (EBA/GL/2020/06 §5.2). Credit-granting criteria must set acceptable debt service coverage and interest coverage limits (Annex 1, items 14-15).
Internal calibration: the debt service coverage ratio (EBITDA / annual debt service, principal plus interest, all lenders) should be at least 1.5. Between 1.0 and 1.5 the file is acceptable only with strong guarantees. Below 1.0 the request is outside risk appetite. The interest coverage ratio (EBITDA / interest expense) should be at least 3.0.

## 4. Profitability and growth
Regulatory requirement: the assessment must consider net operating income and profitability and the realism of financial projections, which should be based on projecting historical data forward; when projections are unreliable the bank must build its own (EBA/GL/2020/06 §5.2).
Internal calibration: negative net income or negative equity requires a recovery plan. Revenue growth above 100% in one year is unusual and must be explained and supported by contracts or audited accounts.

## 5. Financial history
Regulatory requirement: the credit file must contain the borrower's annual financial statements and the documents listed in BAM Directive 1/G/2005 (see POL-CRD-05 §1); EBA/GL/2020/06 Annex 2 asks for financial statements covering a reasonable period, single entity and consolidated.
Internal calibration: at least two years of financial statements are required for credit facilities above 5,000,000 MAD. Exceptions require Credit Committee approval.

## 6. Exposure relative to size
Regulatory requirement: credit-granting criteria must set maximum loan amounts and risk-based limits (EBA/GL/2020/06 Annex 1, items 8 and 12).
Internal calibration: requested exposure above 50% of annual revenue requires Credit Committee approval; above 100% of annual revenue the request is outside risk appetite unless it finances an asset whose own cash flows repay it (project or asset finance).

## 7. Debt to EBITDA and leveraged transactions
Regulatory requirement: banks must define leveraged transactions and an appetite for them (EBA/GL/2020/06 §4.3). The ECB treats as leveraged any exposure where the borrower's total debt exceeds 4.0 times EBITDA after the financing, or a borrower owned by a financial sponsor; total debt above 6.0 times EBITDA should remain exceptional, be duly justified and involve senior management and the risk function (ECB Guidance on leveraged transactions, 2017). Leveraged borrowers should be able to repay all debt, or at least 50% of it, within 5 to 7 years.
Internal calibration: total financial debt / EBITDA above 4.0 flags the file as a leveraged transaction; above 6.0 the request is outside risk appetite except with Credit Committee and Chief Risk Officer approval.

## 8. Sensitivity analysis
Regulatory requirement: for medium-sized and large enterprises, the bank must test the sustainability of repayment under severe but plausible adverse conditions: decline in revenue or margins, failure of a key counterparty, operational loss, reputational damage, liquidity outflow, foreign exchange risk, sector or macroeconomic downturn, increase in funding costs (EBA/GL/2020/06 §5.2).
Internal calibration: the DSCR is recomputed with EBITDA reduced by 20% and interest rates increased by 200 basis points; a stressed DSCR below 1.0 requires additional guarantees or a reduced exposure.

## 9. Concentration and group exposure
Regulatory requirement: the bank's weighted exposure to a single beneficiary — an individual or a group of related persons linked by control or economic dependence — must not exceed 20% of its net own funds (BAM Circular 8/G/2012, maximum risk-division ratio). Exposures of 5% of own funds or more must be declared to Bank Al-Maghrib (BAM Circular 57/DCEC/2001). The bank must manage concentration by counterparty, group, sector and geography (BAM Directive 48/G/2007). If the borrower belongs to a group, creditworthiness is assessed at both entity and group level (EBA/GL/2020/06 §5.2).
Internal calibration: the total exposure to the borrower's group, including the new request, is checked against an internal single-name limit of 15% of the bank's net own funds; requests that would exceed it require Board risk committee approval.

## 10. Collateral and guarantees
Regulatory requirement: collateral and guarantees mitigate credit risk but must not substitute for repayment capacity; the bank must assess guarantees, covenants and negative pledges, and the creditworthiness of guarantors in proportion to the guarantee (EBA/GL/2020/06 §5.2). Collateral must be valued at origination by an independent valuer with no conflict of interest and revalued periodically (EBA/GL/2020/06 §7).
Internal calibration: real estate collateral is valued by an approved external valuer, with a site visit, at origination and at least every 3 years (every year for high-risk files); the same valuer may not value the same property more than twice in a row.
