# POL-CRD-05 — Credit File, External Checks, Decision and Monitoring

> Regulatory basis: BAM Directive no. 1/G/2005 of 1 April 2005 on the minimum information required in credit files; BAM Directive no. 2/W/16 of 10 June 2016 on counterparties belonging to groups; Bank Al-Maghrib credit risk centralisation service (SCR, delegated to licensed credit bureaux); BAM Circular no. 19/G/2002 on loan classification and provisioning and the replacement circular adopted by the Credit Institutions Committee on 5 December 2025; BAM Circular no. 4/W/2014 on internal control; BAM Directive no. 5/W/2021 on climate and environmental financial risks; EBA Guidelines on loan origination and monitoring (EBA/GL/2020/06) §4.3, §5.3 and §8.

## 1. Minimum documents in the credit file
Regulatory requirement (BAM Directive 1/G/2005): for a société anonyme or SCA of any size, and for a SARL, SNC or SCS with turnover above 50 million MAD excluding tax, the file must contain the annual financial statements (états de synthèse) under Moroccan accounting rules, the statutory auditor's report, the minutes of the general meeting that approved the accounts, and the receipt of filing of the statements and auditor's report at the commercial court registry (art. 1). Companies making public offerings also provide half-year statements with the auditor's limited review; listed companies in the first compartment with subsidiaries provide consolidated statements under international standards (art. 2). For a SARL, SNC or SCS with turnover of 50 million MAD or less and total credit of 2 million MAD or less: annual statements, a certificate of regularity and sincerity from a qualified accountant when one is used, the filing receipt and the general meeting minutes (art. 3). Other legal entities and individuals with a business activity whose total credit reaches 2 million MAD provide their statements and the auditor's report or accountant's certificate (art. 4-5). Every page of the balance sheet and income statement must carry the company's stamp and the signature of an authorised officer, and the stamp of the auditor or accountant (art. 6).
Internal calibration: a file that does not meet these minimums cannot be presented for decision.

## 2. Counterparties belonging to large groups
Regulatory requirement (BAM Directive 2/W/16): for an entity belonging to a group whose total bank debt is 500 million MAD or more, the file must also contain the group's annual consolidated statements under international standards and information on the group's bank debt, and the credit file must be updated at least once a year (as reported in the press; check the directive's text before relying on the update date).
Internal calibration: the analyst reconciles the group's bank debt from the consolidated statements with the credit bureau report; a material difference is escalated.

## 3. Credit bureau report
Regulatory requirement: before granting any credit, the bank must obtain the solvency report (rapport de solvabilité) of the client from a delegate of Bank Al-Maghrib's credit risk centralisation service (Creditinfo Maroc or Quantik Maroc).
Internal calibration: the report is also obtained for guarantors and for shareholders holding 25% or more. Arrears, restructured loans or loans classified as non-performing at another bank are escalated to the Credit Committee.

## 4. Credit decision and conflicts of interest
Regulatory requirement: credit decisions follow a documented delegation framework that sets the powers and limits of each decision-maker and of any automated model; a person with a personal, professional, economic or political link to the borrower must not take part in the decision (EBA/GL/2020/06 §4.3). The decision must be documented, state its conditions and risk mitigants and a maximum validity period, and funds are disbursed only once all conditions precedent are met (§5.3).
Internal calibration: the automated due diligence report is decision support; the decision is taken by the authorised credit decision-maker or committee. Any override of the system's recommendation is justified in writing.

## 5. Automated models
Regulatory requirement: when automated models support creditworthiness assessment or decisions, the bank must understand their methodology, data, assumptions and limitations, detect and prevent bias, ensure traceability and auditability of inputs and outputs, monitor model performance, and keep override and escalation procedures (EBA/GL/2020/06 §4.3.4).
Internal calibration: every finding produced by the system cites the data and the policy section it relies on, and the audit log is kept with the credit file.

## 6. Monitoring and early warning indicators
Regulatory requirement: the bank must monitor payment behaviour, covenants, collateral and the borrower's financial position throughout the life of the credit, run periodic credit reviews proportionate to risk, and use early warning indicators with trigger levels, escalation and a watch list (EBA/GL/2020/06 §8).
Internal calibration: early warning indicators include a payment more than 30 days late, a covenant breach, a fall of more than 20% in revenue or EBITDA, a new sanctions, PEP or adverse media alert, a change of shareholder or director, and a downgrade in the credit bureau report.

## 7. Loan classification
Regulatory requirement: loans are classified as performing (saines), sensitive (sensibles) or non-performing (en souffrance); non-performing loans are pre-doubtful (pré-douteuses), doubtful (douteuses) or compromised (compromises), historically based on arrears of more than 90, 180 and 360 days and qualitative criteria, with minimum provisions of 20%, 50% and 100% net of eligible guarantees (BAM Circular 19/G/2002). The reform adopted on 5 December 2025 widens the definition of default (including persistent overdraft excesses beyond 90 days), creates the sensitive class with a minimum specific provision of 10% net of guarantees, and revises the treatment of restructured loans.
Internal calibration: the classification criteria and dates in force must be taken from the official text of the December 2025 circular before use in production.

## 8. Climate and environmental risk
Regulatory requirement: credit institutions must integrate climate and environmental financial risks in their credit risk management (BAM Directive 5/W/2021) and collect information on large borrowers' exposure to these risks (BAM Directive 2/W/2025). Borrowers with higher environmental, social or governance risk require a more in-depth analysis of their business model (EBA/GL/2020/06 §5.2).
Internal calibration: borrowers in high-emission or climate-exposed sectors (for example energy, cement, heavy industry, agriculture dependent on water) provide information on their emissions, transition plan and physical risk exposure.
