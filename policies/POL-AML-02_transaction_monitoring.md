# POL-AML-02 — Transaction Monitoring and Suspicious Activity

> MOCK internal policy for demonstration. Thresholds are illustrative and must be replaced by your bank's calibrated scenarios.

## 1. Cash deposit structuring
Repeated cash deposits just below the internal reporting threshold of 100,000 MAD (between 90,000 and 99,999 MAD) are a typical structuring indicator. Three or more such deposits within any 30-day window must generate an alert. Deposits made across several branches increase the severity.

## 2. Cash intensity
When cash deposits exceed 30% of the declared annual revenue of a company whose activity is not normally cash based, the analyst must obtain a justification of the origin of funds.

## 3. Turnover consistency
Total account credits over 12 months should be consistent with declared revenue. Credits exceeding 150% of declared annual revenue must be explained (shareholder current account contributions, loans, asset sales) with supporting documents.

## 4. Pass-through (rapid movement of funds)
An incoming international transfer followed within 3 days by outgoing transfers of 90% or more of the amount, with no apparent economic link, is a pass-through indicator often associated with layering.

## 5. High-risk jurisdictions
Transfers to or from jurisdictions subject to a FATF call for action, or listed as high-secrecy jurisdictions in the bank's country risk list, require enhanced review of the counterparty and the underlying contract or invoice.

## 6. Round-amount transfers
A high proportion of international transfers in round amounts (multiples of 50,000 MAD) without matching invoices is an indicator of non-commercial flows.

## 7. Suspicious transaction reports
When the analysis gives reasonable grounds to suspect that funds come from an offence or are linked to terrorist financing, Compliance must assess the filing of a suspicious transaction report (déclaration de soupçon) to the national financial intelligence authority (ANRF). The decision to file belongs to the Compliance Officer, never to an automated system. The customer must not be informed (no tipping-off).
